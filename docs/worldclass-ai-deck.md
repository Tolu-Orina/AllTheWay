# World-Class AI Presentation Deck Generation: State of the Art and a Redesign of Your document-cell Loop

## TL;DR
- **Your architecture is one generation behind the research frontier on two specific, fixable points**: (1) your Graph RAG retrieves *descriptions* of past slides to inspire from-scratch geometry, whereas the winning paradigm (PPTAgent, EMNLP 2025; RALF, CVPR 2024; SlideCoder, EMNLP 2025) retrieves *real structured reference slides and edits their actual geometry*; and (2) you have no deterministic pre-render validation layer, whereas the best 2025-2026 systems (AeSlides, LaySPA, Layout-Corrector, Beautiful.ai) catch overlap/overflow/imbalance with cheap code *before or instead of* a vision judge.
- **Split your single 0-100 judge score into three independent dimensions** — Content, Design, Coherence — exactly as PPTEval does, and move purely structural checks (overlap, overflow, off-canvas, aspect ratio, imbalance) *out* of the VLM entirely into deterministic code, because current research shows VLM judges "can rank but cannot score" reliably and have documented "aesthetic blind spots for slide layouts."
- **The highest-leverage redesign is a hybrid**: keep your Planner/Worker/Judge loop but insert a deterministic constraint-and-repair layer between Planner and Worker, convert Graph RAG to structural retrieve-and-edit, and replace the blended judge with dimension-separated scoring plus a verifiable-metrics gate. This mirrors how every constraint-engine product (Beautiful.ai Smart Slides) and every top research system now works.

## Key Findings

**1. The dominant academic paradigm is "retrieve a real slide, then edit its geometry" — not "generate geometry from scratch."** PPTAgent (Zheng et al., EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2501.03936) reframes generation as editing: it clusters reference slides into functional types, extracts content schemas, selects a reference slide per target slide, renders it to HTML, and emits executable edit actions (`replace_span`, `replace_image`, `clone_paragraph`, `del_span`, `del_image`) that "preserve their well-designed layouts and styles." Its formal contrast is explicit: conventional generation is `S = f(C)` (produce all elements with positions from content), while PPTAgent is `A = g(C, Rⱼ)` (produce edit actions against reference slide Rⱼ). It reports "an average score of 3.67 for the three dimensions in PPTEval." This is precisely the gap in your system — your Planner is still computing `f(C)` every turn.

**2. Retrieval works best when the unit retrieved is a whole structured layout, and descriptive/shallow retrieval measurably underperforms.** RALF (Retrieval-Augmented Layout Transformer, CVPR 2024 Oral, arXiv:2311.13602) states: "We show that a simple retrieval augmentation can significantly improve the generation quality. Our model ... retrieves nearest neighbor layout examples based on an input image and feeds these results into an autoregressive generator." ReLayout and PosterO (2025) argue current LLM layout methods are "limited to outputting coordinate information at the element-level ... lacking the structural-level organization." The lesson for your Graph RAG: retrieve the actual box geometry as an editable starting point, not a screenshot to look at.

**3. Deterministic, programmatic layout validation is now proven to beat VLM scoring on structural issues — cheaper, faster, and more accurate.** AeSlides (arXiv:2604.22840, 2026) built four *programmatically verifiable* metrics — aspect-ratio compliance, whitespace ratio, element collision, visual imbalance — and showed they detect layout faults far better than a frontier VLM (aspect-ratio detection F1 0.83 vs GPT-5.2's 0.24), at ~4 s and ~$0 per 50k samples vs ~8 s and ~$200 for the VLM. Training a model against these deterministic rewards moved aspect-ratio compliance from 36%→85% and cut element collisions 43% and whitespace 44%. This is direct, quantitative evidence for your core hypothesis: a cheap deterministic layer should prevent/repair structural failures rather than relying on the vision Judge.

**4. VLM-as-judge is reliable for *relative ranking* and *subjective* dimensions but unreliable for *absolute structural scoring* — which argues for exactly the split you suspect.** A 2026 study ("VLM Judges Can Rank but Cannot Score," arXiv:2604.25235) found evaluation-uncertainty intervals cover ~40-70% of the score range and identifies "ranking-scoring decoupling." AeSlides states plainly that "current VLMs exhibit notable aesthetic blind spots for slide layouts." Meanwhile PPTEval demonstrates that *dimension-separated* MLLM scoring aligns well with humans (avg Pearson 0.71) when each dimension has an explicit 1-5 rubric. Conclusion: keep the VLM for Content and Design/aesthetics (dimension-separated, rubric-anchored), but move structural coherence to deterministic code.

**5. Every commercial "good layout by construction" product uses constraint/rule engines, not post-hoc judging.** Beautiful.ai's Smart Slides make bad layouts structurally difficult by constraining placement to adaptive templates that "automatically realign, resize, and rebalance as you edit" (there are "over 300 Smart Slide layouts"); Microsoft PowerPoint Designer/Copilot Design Suggestions matches content to a finite cloud library of professionally-designed layouts (and notably *fails* when a slide has free-form shapes/text boxes rather than placeholders). The trade-off is control, but the reliability lesson stands.

## Details

### Area 1 — Academic state of the art in slide/presentation generation

**PPTAgent + PPTEval (Zheng et al., EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2501.03936).** Two stages:
- *Stage I — Presentation Analysis:* Segments a reference deck into structural slides (opening, section, closing — identified via long-context LLM over slide text) and content slides (bullet, image, etc. — clustered by rendering slides to images and hierarchical clustering, then MLLM labels layout patterns per cluster). Then *schema extraction* represents each slide element by `{category, description, content}`.
- *Stage II — Generation:* Outline generation picks, per new slide, a reference slide + relevant document content. Slide generation renders the reference to HTML and emits edit-API actions, executed in a REPL with a self-correction loop (max 2 retries) that reads execution feedback.
- *PPTEval:* MLLM-as-judge (GPT-4o), scoring on a **1-5 scale across three independent dimensions**. Critically, PPTEval first generates a *textual description* of each slide along dimension-specific axes, then a separate judge scores from that description. Full rubrics (verbatim, per the paper's appendix):
  - **Content** (averaged across slides): 1 = significant grammatical errors/poorly structured; 3 = "clear and complete but lacks visual aids"; 5 = "well-developed with a clear focus, and the images and text effectively complement each other." Judge inspects amount of information, clarity/language quality, and image relevance.
  - **Design** (averaged across slides): 1 = "conflict between slide styles, making the content difficult to read"; 3 = "basic color scheme ... lacks supplementary visual elements ... making it look plain"; 5 = "harmonious and engaging ... visual elements ... enhance the slide's overall visual appeal." Judge inspects color contrast/harmony, visual elements, and adherence to design principles such as "avoiding overlapping elements."
  - **Coherence** (assessed at presentation level): 1 = inconsistent terminology/unclear logic; 3 = "logical structure is sound with fluent transitions; however, it lacks basic background information"; 5 = "narrative structure is engaging and meticulously organized with detailed and comprehensive background information."
  - Agreement: avg Pearson 0.71 / Spearman 0.74 vs humans (Design highest at 0.90/0.88, Coherence lowest at 0.55/0.57); human inter-rater Fleiss' κ avg 0.59. Best PPTAgent config scored Content 3.28 / Design 3.27 / Coherence 4.48 (avg 3.67 across dimensions).
- Note the design pattern: PPTAgent's judge (PPTEval) is used for *evaluation*, while robustness during generation comes from the code-execution self-correction loop — i.e., they separate "is the code valid" (deterministic REPL feedback) from "is it good" (MLLM). Your system currently collapses both onto the vision Judge.

**AutoPresent + SlidesBench (Ge et al., CVPR 2025, arXiv:2501.00912).** Program-generation approach: NL instruction → Python program (via a high-level `SlidesLib` API: add_title, add_text, image search/generation, etc.) → executed to an editable slide. Key documented findings: (a) programmatic/code methods produce higher-quality, editable slides than end-to-end image generation (Stable Diffusion, DALL-E); (b) an 8B fine-tuned Llama (AutoPresent) reaches parity with GPT-4o; (c) **iterative self-refinement improves quality in all settings**; (d) a **documented design-quality gap remains vs human-made slides** — the paper states: "While GPT-4o can produce reasonable slides, it still exhibits a substantial gap in design quality compared to human-generated slides." SlidesBench is "the first benchmark for slide generation with 7k training and 585 testing examples derived from 310 slide decks across 10 domains," with both reference-based and reference-free evaluation. Limitation acknowledged: single-slide, single-pass, no deck-level workflow.

**Talk to Your Slides (Jung et al., ACL Findings 2026, arXiv:2505.11604).** Not generation but *editing*: an LLM agent edits live PowerPoint via COM using a two-level hierarchy — high-level LLM interprets instruction and plans, low-level Python manipulates the object model directly (not pixels). Reported 34% faster, 34.8% better instruction fidelity, 87% cheaper than GUI/image agents. Contributes TSBench (379 editing instructions). The architectural lesson relevant to you: **operate on structured object models, not rasterized images, whenever the task is structural.**

**Newer 2025-2026 work that supersedes/extends PPTAgent & AutoPresent:**
- **SlideCoder** (Tang et al., EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2506.07964): reference-image → editable slide via *layout-aware, RAG-enhanced hierarchical* code generation using python-pptx, with a Color-Gradient Segmentation algorithm and Hierarchical RAG; beats baselines by up to 40.5 points; releases SlideMaster (7B). Introduces Slide2Code benchmark with a Slide Complexity Metric. Directly relevant: it combines *layout-aware prompting + RAG* and reports the from-scratch NL baselines "frequently produce slides with overlapping elements or content that extends beyond canvas boundaries."
- **AeSlides** (Pan et al., 2026, arXiv:2604.22840): verifiable-reward RL — details in Area 3.
- **DeepPresenter** (Zheng et al., 2026, arXiv:2602.22839): "environment-grounded reflection for agentic presentation generation" (from the same PPTAgent group); uses a Vendi Score (DINOv2 feature-similarity eigenvalue entropy) for diversity.
- Other 2026 benchmarks/systems in this space: DeckBench (multi-agent academic slide gen/editing), SlideBot (multi-agent), SlideTailor, PPTBench, X+Slides (audience-conditioned), ECHO (schema-constrained co-editing with a human-inspected JSON operation plan before any change — explicitly criticizes free-form edit generation for having "no architectural guarantee that malformed or semantically impossible commands will be rejected before execution").

### Area 2 — Content-aware layout generation (broader)

- **LayoutGPT** (Feng et al., NeurIPS 2023): LLM emits layouts as structured CSS/HTML-style representations; showed LLMs have latent design ability via structured-language output.
- **LayoutPrompter** (Lin et al., NeurIPS 2023, arXiv:2311.06495): training-free, uses **dynamic exemplar selection** (retrieval of similar layouts for in-context learning) + a ranker; surpasses training-based baselines. Represents each element as an HTML `<div>`. Limitation (per ReLayout): cannot take a canvas image as input; element-level coordinates only.
- **RALF** (Horita et al., CVPR 2024 Oral, arXiv:2311.13602): retrieves K=16 nearest-neighbor real layouts by image similarity, cross-attends them into an autoregressive generator. Ablations show retrieval significantly improves FID, alignment, overlap; "Top-1 Retrieval" (retrieve-only, no generation) is a documented baseline that "almost disregards the given content" — i.e., pure retrieval is insufficient, and pure generation is data-starved; the win is retrieval-*augmented* generation. **This is the single most important reference for redesigning your Graph RAG.**
- **CAL-RAG** (Forouzandehmehr et al., SIGIR GENNEXT 2025, arXiv:2506.21934): retrieval-augmented *multi-agent* content-aware layout: (a) LLM layout recommender proposes structured element placements from retrieved exemplars, (b) a vision-language grader agent scores with visual metrics, (c) a feedback agent gives targeted refinements; implemented in LangGraph, SOTA on PKU PosterLayout for underlay effectiveness, alignment, overlap; beats LayoutPrompter. This is essentially a more mature version of your Planner/Judge loop *with retrieval of real layouts* and a feedback agent that proposes corrections.
- **LaySPA** (Li et al., Adobe/Virginia Tech, arXiv:2509.16891 / "From Pixels to Policies" arXiv:2602.13912): RL (GRPO) that gives an LLM agent explicit spatial reasoning over a *serialized textual canvas* (geometry, element attributes, inter-element relations), with a **hybrid reward = format correctness + structural constraints (boundary adherence, non-overlap, size consistency) + visual quality (alignment, spacing, hierarchy, distribution)**. Produces interpretable `<think>` reasoning + structured JSON. Outperforms larger general-purpose LLMs and rivals specialized models. Shows structural constraints can be *learned* as rewards — an alternative to hard-coding them.
- **VASCAR** (Zhang et al., LY Corp/Osaka, arXiv:2412.04237): LVLM iteratively self-corrects layout by looking at *rendered* colored bounding boxes on the canvas — analogous to your Judge seeing PNGs, but the corrector is the same model that edits (unlike your Judge, which can only score). Evidence that letting the visual critic *also propose the fix* helps.
- **PosterLlama** (Seol et al., 2024): fine-tunes a MLLM (DINOv2 + CodeLlama) to emit SVG `<rect>` layouts; SOTA visual-textual coherence; shows structural/code representation + visual grounding beats coordinate-only.
- **Diffusion layout models:** LayoutDM (CVPR 2023), DLT (joint discrete-continuous diffusion transformer), and **Layout-Corrector** (Iwai et al., ECCV 2024, arXiv:2409.16689). Layout-Corrector is conceptually important for you: it's a *separate learned module* that, during diffusion generation, scores each token's correctness, **masks low-scoring (erroneous) tokens, and regenerates only those**, fixing the "layout sticking" problem where a model can't correct its own inharmonious placements. This is the diffusion analog of "detect the bad box and fix just that box" — cheaper and more targeted than regenerating the whole layout, which is exactly what your fresh-Planner-every-turn approach fails to do.

**Unit-of-retrieval synthesis:** across RALF, LayoutPrompter, CAL-RAG, and PPTAgent, the consistent finding is that retrieving **whole structured layouts (real element geometry / editable schemas)** and adapting them beats retrieving descriptions or style tokens. Descriptive retrieval underperforms because (a) it discards the exact spatial relationships that make a layout work, forcing the generator to re-derive geometry it can't reliably produce, and (b) it provides no editable substrate — the model must still solve the hard from-scratch placement problem. Pure retrieval also underperforms (RALF's Top-1 baseline) because it ignores the new content; the win is retrieval as an *editable prior*.

### Area 3 — Constraint-based / deterministic layout engines

**Beautiful.ai Smart Slides / DesignerBot.** Publicly documented (marketing + reviews; internal algorithm is proprietary and *not* publicly disclosed — stated explicitly to avoid speculation): Smart Slides are adaptive templates with "built-in design rules" that "automatically realign, resize, and rebalance as you edit," handling "spacing, alignment, and visual hierarchy" and "chart layout" so that "it is difficult to create cluttered layouts." There are "over 300 Smart Slide layouts." Users cannot freely drag elements to arbitrary positions — the engine "decides where things go based on its internal rules." The product launched with "Smart Slides — templates that automatically reformat as you add or edit content," raised $11 million in Series A funding by 2020, and added DesignerBot in 2022. DesignerBot (LLM layer, powered by Anthropic per Beautiful.ai's site) generates outline → content → picks the appropriate Smart Slide type per section → populates it; "all governed by Beautiful.ai's design rules." Beautiful.ai's own positioning (Google Workspace Marketplace listing) captures the philosophy: "We believe that the only way to guarantee good presentation design is to build the intelligence of a designer directly into the tool ... It knows what's on your slide, what you might add later, and how to adjust the layout when you do." The precise geometric algorithms, constraint solver, and overlap/contrast checks are **not publicly documented** — no engineering blog or patent text detailing the solver was found.

**Microsoft PowerPoint Designer / Copilot "Design Suggestions."** Designer is a "layout suggestion engine that works with visual objects" — object detection + content classification matched against a cloud library of professionally-designed layout templates; "Designer does not read the words in your text boxes" (per third-party analysis). Documented failure/trigger conditions reveal the rule-based nature: it won't suggest when a slide "has a shape or text box drawn on it" (vs a placeholder), when there are >4 images, images <200×200 px, extra objects on process/picture slides, or when the deck is co-authored. Copilot renamed Designer to "Design Suggestions" and adds LLM content generation; the design engine itself is unchanged and combines "large language models ... and Microsoft Designer's visual algorithms." Internal algorithms are proprietary/undisclosed.

**Other products (Canva Magic Design, Tome, Gamma, Plus AI):** All market AI + auto-layout, but none publish technical detail on geometry validation; their internals are proprietary and I found no patents/engineering talks specifying overlap detection, grid snapping, or font-metric measurement. Do not assume specifics.

**The strongest *published* deterministic layer is AeSlides' verifiable metric suite** (arXiv:2604.22840) — this is your best concrete blueprint for a pre-render validator, computed from rendered HTML/DOM via Playwright:
- **Aspect-ratio compliance:** dynamically measure rendered width:height vs 16:9 (init viewport 1280×10, reset to measured scrollHeight, screenshot); anti-hack by normalizing CSS (strip `min-height`, `overflow:hidden`) via cssutils.
- **Excessive whitespace:** grayscale → local-variance map via box filters (neighborhood H=201, W=151) → clip/normalize → binarize at τ=0.05, drop boundary regions, compute content vs whitespace area ratio; anti-hack by removing background images + Gaussian blur (kernel=21) to defeat texture tricks.
- **Element collision:** from DOM tree + bounding boxes, extract "visual units" (filter invisible/non-semantic/fine-grained nodes), then detect (i) overlapping boxes between non-ancestor elements, (ii) elements escaping parent containers, (iii) elements overflowing the slide boundary; exclude benign cases (watermarks, corner badges) unless they occlude main content; produce a reweighted collision score.
- **Visual imbalance:** compute visual centroid of all units, measure ellipse-normalized offset from canvas center d = √[((x−x_c)/x_tol)² + ((y−y_c)/y_tol)²] with x_tol=0.05, y_tol=0.15 (horizontal imbalance weighted more).
- Detection accuracy (F1): aspect 0.83, whitespace 0.80, collision 0.47, imbalance 0.61 — all far above the frontier-VLM baseline; ~4 s, ~$0. Turned into GRPO rewards via smoothstep-clipped shaping (monotonic metrics: `R=1 if x≤lower; 0 if x≥upper; 3u²−2u³ otherwise`) and an asymmetric quadratic for aspect ratio, with per-component reward normalization (following GDPO) to prevent reward collapse across differing metric scales.

**Layout-Corrector (ECCV 2024)** provides the "detect-and-repair-only-the-bad-parts" pattern in learned form; LaySPA provides the "structural constraints as reward" pattern. Together with AeSlides these establish that structural quality is best handled by explicit, checkable rules — not a blended vision score.

### Area 4 — VLM-as-judge design for visual/design quality

- **Dimension separation beats a blended score.** PPTEval's three-dimension design (with per-dimension rubrics and a describe-then-score two-step) is the reference implementation and correlates with humans at avg Pearson 0.71. A blended single number suffers "criteria entanglement bias" / halo effect (per "Curse of Knowledge," arXiv:2509.03419), where judges assign similar scores across entangled dimensions, defeating diagnosis — exactly the problem you describe with your one 0-100 number.
- **VLM judges rank better than they score.** "VLM Judges Can Rank but Cannot Score" (arXiv:2604.25235) documents "ranking-scoring decoupling": high ranking correlation but wide, uninformative absolute-score intervals (covering ~40% of range for aesthetics, ~70% for chart/math). Implication: a hard numeric gate like "≥95" on a VLM score is fragile; prefer pairwise/relative judgments (is turn N better than turn N-1?) or coarse rubric bands, and reserve fine numeric thresholds for deterministic metrics.
- **Documented failure modes** (across InteractScience arXiv:2510.09724, VIABLE arXiv:2605.31351, and surveys): numerical misreading, stylistic confusion (mistaking color/thickness for errors), partial-credit blindness, **layout sensitivity (penalizing valid alternative layouts)**, self-preference bias (a judge favoring its own family's outputs — measured as high as 94.2% in one benchmark), position bias, verbosity bias. AeSlides specifically found VLMs have "aesthetic blind spots for slide layouts" — sometimes worse than random on structural issues.
- **Mitigations / best practices:** self-consistency (repeat with different seeds and aggregate); multi-judge ensembles across model families; use a *different* model family for judging than for generation (defeats self-preference); explicit rubrics with concrete evidence per criterion rather than vague descriptors; position/length normalization; describe-then-score decomposition (PPTEval); calibration to human distributions (LLM-Rubric).
- **Should the judge also propose corrections?** The evidence is split by task: for *structural* issues, no — use deterministic detectors that can be auto-repaired (AeSlides, Layout-Corrector) or a corrector agent that edits (CAL-RAG's feedback agent, VASCAR). For *subjective* content/design, a judge that returns *targeted, dimension-tagged issues* (as yours does) is useful, but those issues should feed an editor that can act on them, not just cap a score. Your current Judge "cannot rewrite the IR," which wastes its diagnostic output — the CAL-RAG pattern (grader → feedback agent → refinement) closes that loop.

### Area 5 — Architecture patterns compared

| Pattern | How structural quality is ensured | Content/aesthetic quality | Cost/latency | Editability | Representative systems | Key weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Free-form generate → judge (your current loop)** | Post-hoc, probabilistic, via vision judge after full render | Vision judge (blended) | High (full render + VLM every turn) | High (native IR) | Your document-cell; naive GPT-4o slide gen | Structural faults caught late/expensively; blended score can't diagnose; Planner re-derives geometry each turn |
| **Constraint / rule engine** | By construction — bad layouts near-impossible | Fixed template aesthetics; limited novelty | Very low (deterministic) | Low (can't free-place) | Beautiful.ai Smart Slides, PPT Designer | Rigid; limited creative range; proprietary |
| **Retrieval-and-edit** | Inherited from real reference slide geometry; edits are local | High (reuses human-designed layouts) | Medium (retrieval + edit actions) | High (structured edits) | PPTAgent, RALF, SlideCoder, LayoutPrompter | Needs a good structured corpus; retrieval quality gates output |
| **Deterministic-verify + repair** | Explicit checkable metrics; auto-repair or mask-and-regen | Orthogonal (pairs with any generator) | Low (code) + optional regen | High | AeSlides, Layout-Corrector, LaySPA (as reward) | Doesn't create content; needs geometry access |
| **Hybrid (recommended)** | Retrieve real layout → edit → deterministic validate/repair → dimension-separated judge | Dimension-separated VLM (Content, Design) + verifiable metrics (structure) | Medium, but fewer wasted full renders | High | CAL-RAG (grader+feedback); your proposed redesign | More components to build/orchestrate |

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Immediate, low-risk (weeks): split the score and add a deterministic gate.**
1. **Decompose the Judge into three independent scores** (Content, Design, Coherence) using PPTEval's rubrics and its describe-then-score two-step. Store all three; never collapse to one number for gating. Gate on per-dimension thresholds (e.g., Content ≥4/5 AND Design ≥4/5 AND structural gate passed) rather than a blended ≥95.
2. **Build a deterministic pre-render validator** that runs on the Planner's IR *before* the Worker paints, porting AeSlides' four checks to your box geometry (you already have x/y/w/h in the IR, so you don't even need to rasterize first): (a) **overlap** — pairwise bounding-box intersection among non-ancestor elements; (b) **off-canvas / container escape** — box exceeds slide or parent bounds; (c) **text overflow** — measure rendered text against real font metrics (see step 4); (d) **aspect/margin/grid** — snap to a grid and enforce margins; (e) **visual imbalance** — centroid offset with x_tol=0.05, y_tol=0.15; (f) **contrast** — WCAG contrast ratio between text and its background fill/image. Emit machine-readable violations (element id + violation type + magnitude).
3. **Move the "missing planned image caps score at 79" rule into this deterministic layer** as a hard validation failure, not a judge instruction — it's a checkable fact, not an aesthetic judgment.

**Stage 2 — Core redesign (1-2 months): add repair and convert retrieval.**
4. **Insert an auto-repair step** between validator and Worker. For each violation, apply cheap deterministic fixes first (nudge to grid, shrink font within min-size, reflow to margin, push apart overlapping boxes), then re-validate. Only escalate to an LLM/Planner edit for violations code can't resolve. This is the Layout-Corrector pattern (fix only the bad elements) and avoids your current "regenerate all geometry from scratch each turn." For text-overflow specifically, measure with real font metrics: in Python use `Pillow`'s `ImageFont.getbbox()` / `getlength()`, or PIL+`fontTools`, or measure via the same LibreOffice/headless renderer you already run; in JS use `canvas measureText` or the pptxgenjs text-sizing path. Shrink-to-fit or reflow before render.
5. **Convert Graph RAG from descriptive to structural retrieve-and-edit.** Change the corpus unit from "coordinate/description JSON + screenshots (inspiration)" to **actual structured, editable reference slide layouts** (the real box geometry + element schema, PPTAgent-style `{category, description, content, x, y, w, h}`). Retrieve nearest neighbors by *content schema + slot structure* (how many text/image/chart slots, their functional type), not just brief embedding — RALF retrieves by layout/appearance similarity, PPTAgent selects by functional slide type. Then have the Planner emit **edit actions against the retrieved layout's real geometry** (replace/resize/duplicate slots) instead of computing all boxes from scratch. Keep the deck-graph prev/next walk, but use it to enforce *cross-slide consistency* of the adapted layouts (shared margins, title positions), not just as inspiration.
6. **Give the Judge's issues somewhere to go.** Adopt the CAL-RAG grader→feedback→refine loop: the dimension-separated Judge returns targeted, dimension-tagged issues; a lightweight editor (or the Planner in edit-mode) acts on Content/Design issues, while structural issues are already handled deterministically. This stops the Judge's output from being wasted on a mere score cap.

**Stage 3 — Advanced (opportunistic): learn the constraints.**
7. If you accumulate enough logged (IR, violations, human-rating) data, consider LaySPA/AeSlides-style GRPO fine-tuning of the Planner using your deterministic metrics as verifiable rewards — AeSlides moved collision −43% and aspect compliance 36%→85% this way. This bakes structural competence into the model so the repair layer fires less often.

**Benchmarks / thresholds that change these recommendations:**
- If your deterministic validator's overlap/overflow catch-rate is already >90% and full-render+judge cost is not your bottleneck, deprioritize Stage 3.
- If retrieval hit-quality is poor (retrieved layouts rarely match target slot structure), fix the corpus/indexing (schema-based retrieval) *before* investing in edit-action machinery — a bad retrieve-and-edit is worse than good from-scratch (cf. RALF's weak Top-1-retrieval-only baseline).
- If your VLM judge scores show wide run-to-run variance (test with self-consistency across ≥3 seeds), switch design/content gating from absolute thresholds to *pairwise* "is this turn better than last?" comparisons, and rely on deterministic metrics for any hard numeric gate.
- Track the three PPTEval dimensions separately over time; if Coherence is your weakest (as it is even for PPTAgent relative to human agreement), invest in the deck-graph cross-slide consistency, not per-slide aesthetics.

## Caveats
- **Proprietary internals are not public.** Beautiful.ai's Smart Slides solver, Microsoft Designer's algorithms, and Canva/Gamma/Tome/Plus AI internals are not disclosed in any patent, engineering blog, or talk I could locate; I have reported only what secondary sources and product docs state and flagged the rest as unknown.
- **Some cited works are very recent (2026) preprints** (AeSlides arXiv:2604.22840, DeepPresenter arXiv:2602.22839, "VLM Judges Can Rank but Cannot Score" arXiv:2604.25235, LaySPA v2 arXiv:2602.13912) and have not all completed peer review; their specific numbers may shift. AeSlides' exact reward hyperparameters (α, β, smoothstep bounds, collision reweighting) are not all numerically specified in the paper text and would need to be read from its code.
- **PPTEval's rubrics were designed for document→deck academic evaluation**; adapt the dimension definitions to your business-deck audience (e.g., "background information" matters less for a sales pitch than a research talk).
- **VLM-judge numbers are task-dependent**; PPTEval's 0.71 human correlation is for its own setting and may not transfer directly to your rendered PNGs and prompts — validate against your own human ratings before trusting any absolute threshold.
- Your existing user-confirmation gate after the story brief (deck.v1) is a genuine strength that several systems (ECHO, Beautiful.ai's outline-first flow) have independently converged on; keep it.
